# Rules diagrams (RC485)

Fifty-seven editable SVG diagrams for the Heraldry Skirmish rulebook, generated deterministically from code (stdlib only): FIG-01 to FIG-19 rules diagrams, and FIG-20 to FIG-57 the scenario deployment maps (legend, 36 Core Scenario maps, 10.1 procedural template).

Regenerate everything (SVGs, `manifest.json`, `placements.json`, `anchor_proposals.json`, `production/qa/scenario-coverage.json`) and run all checks:

    python3 figures/src/make_figures.py          # all figures
    python3 figures/src/make_figures.py FIG-07   # SVG subset only (no manifest rewrite)

Files

- `src/svgkit.py` shared visual grammar for the rules diagrams FIG-01 to FIG-12 and FIG-17 (tokens, terrain patterns, arrows, callouts, legend, grid helpers, text metrics from `src/fontmetrics.json`); it is unchanged by the setup-diagram iconography below.
- `src/scenkit.py` the one iconography for every setup and deployment diagram (FIG-13 to FIG-16, FIG-18 to FIG-57): glyph functions, the glyph registry `REG`, the key swatches and compact key strips. See "Shared iconography" below.
- `src/fig_seq.py`, `fig_spatial.py`, `fig_monster.py`, `fig_scen.py` (FIG-13 to FIG-16), `fig_misc.py` (FIG-17), `fig_appx.py` (FIG-18 and FIG-19) figure drawings; `src/figdata.py` IDs, captions, insertion targets, source references, semantic checks.
- `out/FIG-NN.svg` outputs, 1 user unit = 1 pt, Source Sans 3 text of 9 pt or more, grayscale only, no raster or external references. FIG-01 to FIG-19, FIG-20 (legend) and FIG-57 (template) are `full` scope (504 pt wide); FIG-21 to FIG-56 (the maps) are `column` scope (243 pt wide, height at most about 260 pt).
- `manifest.json` one record per figure; `placements.json` maps block id to `[{id, caption, width, scope}]`, inserted immediately after that block.

Checks run by `make_figures.py`: geometry and arithmetic assertions behind the manifest's semantic checks, SVG validation (grayscale colours, font size >= 9, stroke >= 0.5, no images or hrefs, widths), and placement validation (block exists in `build/blocks.json`, is a top-level paragraph or list, is not in Appendix C, caption ends with a section reference and has at most 3 sentences).

Visual inspection: `python3 figures/src/test/render.py FIG-01 FIG-02 ...` renders PNGs through Typst into `figures/src/test/` (git-ignored; safe to delete).

Visual grammar of the rules diagrams FIG-01 to FIG-12 and FIG-17: Party A = black circle; Party B = white circle with centre dot; Monster = hatched hexagon (hatched square for Large); Blocking = solid dark gray; Cover = cross-hatch; Difficult = dot stipple; Impassable = dense diagonal hatch; solid arrow = movement or permitted; dashed = line of sight; dotted = forced movement; arrow ending in X = not permitted; numbered black circles = steps. Diagrams that are not to scale carry "Schematic — not to scale"; inch values shown are only values the source states.

## Scenario deployment maps (FIG-20 to FIG-57)

- `src/scenario_specs.json` one structured spec per Core Scenario (key such as `"Greenmark 1"`): `tag`, `insert_after_line` (source line of the paragraph the map follows, block id `B` plus 5 digits), `board_line`, `caption`, `detail_figures`, `setup_rules`, `foot` notes, `ambiguities`, `undrawn` (conditional placements deliberately not drawn) and `elements`. Every element has `id`, `kind` (zone, feature, marker, monster, edge), geometry in inches (origin top-left, North drawn at the top, short edges top and bottom), `status` and `lines` (source line numbers inside the scenario's span; `shared` lists 10.1 / 10.6 rule lines such as step 11 at line 11072). Statuses: `MANDATORY_POSITION` (the source fixes the location), `CONSTRAINED` (a range or relation, drawn solid with its dimension line or ring), `ILLUSTRATIVE` (location open: one legal arrangement drawn light grey dashed). Annotations (`anno`) draw and assert the stated distances: `rel` (within / at least / exactly, as ruler or ring), `ring`, `edgein`, `dim`, `pdim` (Hero-to-Hero), `inside`; `hide` asserts without drawing.
- `src/fig_scenmaps.py` the one renderer: board 36"x36" at 6 pt per inch (zones and stated distances to scale, markers enlarged, Large and Huge Monsters at footprint scale), 6" scale bar and N arrow in the title row, W/E/S letters, automatic label placement (scored candidates, haloed text, leader lines), standard note "Schematic. Grey dashed = position not fixed by the rules", plus `fig20` (the shared legend, placed after the 10.4 introduction, which the maps rely on instead of a per-map legend) and `fig57` (10.1 Preparation Steps template, "Template - not a fixed battlefield", with the step 11 default zones and the step 7 exclusion distances 3" and 6").
- `check_scenmaps()` (run by `make_figures.py` selfcheck): exactly one map per 10.4 scenario heading and sequential figure ids in book order; every element cites lines inside its scenario's span; every inch value printed on a map appears in its cited source lines; every `rel`, `ring`, `edgein`, `dim`, `pdim` and `inside` annotation is asserted numerically (closest-point distances, 2.6); markers and Monsters stay out of the deployment zones (10.1 step 8) and Blocking, Impassable or obstacle pieces stay 3" from an edge and 6" from a zone (10.1 step 7) unless flagged `exempt` because the scenario says otherwise; the legend and template inch values are checked against their cited lines.
- `anchor_proposals.json` content-anchor definitions (section, type, text prefix) for FIG-20 to FIG-57, ready to merge into `config/content_anchors.json` so placement survives line renumbering.
- `production/qa/scenario-coverage.json` the coverage matrix: per scenario the map figure, detail figures, insertion block, shared setup rules, element counts by status, conditional placements not drawn, ambiguities logged for an owner decision, and status (`drawn` or `blocked-by-ambiguity` where one element cannot be drawn without inventing a rule).
- Visual grammar additions (maps only): water (waves), slippery (dashes) and a neutral grey "Impassable or Blocking, decided per piece" (2.9.1) swatch; hazard marker (grey square with "!"); other marker (white square with dot); Party A zone has a solid black edge band, Party B an open band, and a zone both parties share a black band with white dashes.

## Shared iconography (setup and deployment diagrams)

- Every symbol on FIG-13 to FIG-16 and FIG-18 to FIG-57 is drawn by a function in `src/scenkit.py` (Hero discs, zones with edge bands, objective / chest / hazard / marker glyphs, Monster hexagon and Large and Huge squares at footprint scale, the unresolved ? tag, terrain patterns, wall lines, Ledge and Crumbling Ledge, dimension lines with end ticks, rings, leader lines, entry and exit arrows, targeting line, step circles, compass, scale bar, grey-dashed "one legal arrangement" state). `fig_scenmaps.py`, `fig_scen.py`, `fig_appx.py` and the 10.1 template all call them; none draws its own symbols.
- Each function wraps its output in `<g data-glyph="ID">` (plus `data-state="illus"` for the grey-dashed state). `scenkit.REG` is the registry of ids (`hero-A`, `zone-AB`, `obj`, `unres`, `ter-chasm`, `ledge-crumbling`, `dim`, `ring`, `leader`, ...) with the key label, the compact-strip label and the swatch drawer. Pattern ids map to registry ids through `scenkit.PATMAP`.
- FIG-20 draws one swatch (inside `<g data-key="ID">`) for every registry entry, using the same functions. `fig_scenmaps.check_registry()` (run by `make_figures.py` after the build) parses the generated SVGs and fails if FIG-20 differs from the registry, if any setup diagram emits a glyph id, state or pattern that the key does not explain, if the key explains an id that no diagram uses, or if the compact key strip (`<g data-strip>`, built by `scenkit.strip_for`) on FIG-13, FIG-14, FIG-15, FIG-16, FIG-18 or FIG-19 does not list exactly the glyphs that figure uses. Appendix A and B readers may not have seen the key in 10.4, which is why those figures carry their own strip.
- Every setup diagram caption ends `; key: FIG-20 (see <section>).` (FIG-18 and FIG-19 keep their appendix section reference).
- The 36 Core maps are drawn at 6 pt per inch (column width, scale bar and compass in the title row); the Appendix A and B boards are drawn by the same renderer from the `appendix` section of `scenario_specs.json` at 7.5 pt per inch (24"x24"), with the Round 1 table, example score and A.11 relative-layout inset beside them. Appendix specs cite only Appendix lines (`spans`), assert every distance, and never cite 10.1 or 10.6 rule lines, so no advanced rule leaks into Basic Mode.
- Elements the scenario text does not place (`kind: "unresolved"`, status `UNRESOLVED`: Undermarches 1 Group 3 arrival edge, Sun-Drowned 3 Tomb Herald, The Mire 2 escape edge, The Mire 5 Group 1 arrival and escape edge) are not drawn on the battlefield; the map shows a ? tag with a short note under it ("... (see text)"), and `production/qa/scenario-coverage.json` lists them under `unresolved_elements`. Zone sizes taken from the 10.1 step 11 default (Sun-Drowned 2 and 3, The Mire 3, 5 and 6) are labelled "(default)". Terrain whose type the scenario does not state (Workbenches, Reed screens, Corrupted Muck, low defensive walls, Deep Sand, Fallen Pillars, Razor Reeds, Dense Reeds, Mangroves) uses the neutral per-piece swatch; Locus pillars and Glyphic Stones (Impassable Terrain and objectives) are objective diamonds on an Impassable square.
- `scenario_specs.json` annotation types added: `cdim` (centre-to-centre dimension), `cring` (centre distance in a range, asserted, not drawn), an `off` offset for dimension lines (drawn with extension lines), element kinds `hero` and `unresolved`, marker field `on` (terrain piece under the glyph), terrain kinds `chasm`, `ledge`, `crumbling` and `coverdiff` (Cover tagged Difficult).
- `check_terrain_types()` (in `check_scenmaps`) maps each named terrain piece to its 2.9.1 standard type (`TERRAIN_2_9_1` in `fig_scenmaps.py`; unlabelled pieces take the name of their labelled sibling) and fails on a mismatch unless the element carries `typed_by_scenario`. `make_figures.py` preserves every existing `review` key in `production/qa/scenario-coverage.json` when it regenerates the file.
