# RC522 lore review: Section 1, The World of Heraldry

Prepared 2026-10-31 for the owner's decision. Nothing here is applied. Each item is an exact replacement against `source/Heraldry_RC522.md` (SHA-256 `fad164ee…4a59e`); the machine-readable twin is `editorial/proposals/rc523-lore-review.json` (status `proposed`). ## Verdict

Section 1 is strong work. The voice is consistent and genuinely grim: short declaratives, ledger-and-contract imagery, institutions that outlived their people, and omens that read like warnings rather than decoration. The six climates, six Bastions, five traditions, and eight legends all cross-reference correctly with the bestiary, the scenarios, the named artifacts, and the campaign chapter. The copy is clean: Canadian spelling is consistent, the Oxford comma is honoured throughout, and there are no repeated words, double spaces, or markup faults.

What remains is one geography contradiction, one timeline contradiction, a handful of sentence-level slips, and a short list of omissions where the rest of the book introduces lore that Section 1 never foreshadows. The largest omission is that the lore shows orcs, goblins, ratkin, and deep elves only as raiders, while 6.9 lets players field them as Heroes.

## Your question: "colder bargains"

You are right that it is awkward. The comparative has nothing to compare against, and the three adjectives before it (old, broken, hungry) are all simple. "Cold bargains" fixes the rhythm and keeps the word that matters: bargains, writs, and tolls are the spine of this setting, and the sentence is announcing that theme. "Cold hearts" is a good phrase but it is generic grimdark; "bargains" is specifically Heraldry. See LORE-B01. Your call; the JSON applies whichever you choose.

## Part 5. Checked and clean

- **Names.** Every Bastion, council, title, legend, dragon, and Boss named in Section 1 is spelled identically wherever it recurs (Chapters 6 to 11, 7.17.3 named artifacts, Appendix C).
- **Legends versus artifacts.** Saint Cael, Ysolde Vane, Mordain Kest, Edrava Salttide, Old Wode, Karnessa Iron-Vein, the Hollow Crown, and the Voice Beneath the Bells agree in every detail with their 7.17.3 item lore and their scenarios (bridge, iron cage, warm reliquaries, counting stone, iron-shod staff, black blood, faceless statues, no grave).
- **Dragons.** The five named dragons sit in the five climates the bestiary gives them; the Ashlands has none, as the lore insists; Khorrun's description matches its Boss entry; the Antlered Warden and Avatar of the Old Growth match Greythorn's tithe lore and Greenmark Scenarios 2 and 6.
- **Traditions.** Arcane Spells, Divine Prayers, Druidic Rituals, Sorceries, and Psychic Abilities are the rules names; Section 1 uses them exactly. The Chapter 8 opener restates 1.3.4 in different words, which is deliberate chapter-lead practice, and nothing in it conflicts.
- **Attitudes to casters.** Sablekeep registers, hangs sorcerers and druids, and quarrels with Greythorn over caster law; Greythorn pays druids; Saltspire mistrusts them; Deepwarden watches shadows. Consistent across 1.3.3, 1.3.4, Chapter 8, and the scenarios.
- **Spelling.** Canadian throughout: armour, centre, defence, grey, mould, honour, neighbours, travelled, fuelled, labelled, with Oxford -ize (recognize, civilization, prioritize). "Artifact" is the Canadian Oxford form and is used consistently as a rules term. No American variants anywhere in the book.
- **Oxford comma.** Present in every list in Section 1. A whole-book regex sweep found 31 candidates; all were compound list items already correctly punctuated.
- **Typography.** No double spaces, straight quotes, repeated words, stray commas, or broken bold-in-italic runs in Section 1.
- **Timeline.** Apart from LORE-C01, every age reference is compatible with a Fall roughly two centuries past: seven Hearth Marshals, a vault sealed two generations, Edrava's grave-house sealed two hundred years, "centuries" for the dead empire's patrols.
- **The glance box.** 1.3 repeats "belongs to the dead and the old powers that waited out the Fall" from the paragraph that follows. This is the summary box doing its job and I do not propose changing it.

## Part 6. Considered and not proposed

- **A pantheon or named saints.** The silence of the gods is the setting's thesis ("The Long Silence"); one named saint, Cael, is enough. Naming more would soften the point.
- **A calendar or a fixed date for the Fall.** 1.3.1 makes the argument over the date part of the grief. Keep it.
- **A currency note** ("no Bastion strikes new coin"). Attractive, but it would create a world fact that later rules text (markets, Retirement Payouts) never acknowledges. Not worth the constraint.
- **Shortening the Khorrun passage.** It is the longest aside in the chapter, but it is also the best-written, and the paragraph split in LORE-A06 solves the readability problem.
