VAULTFANG - CLASSIC FANTASY PEN-AND-INK ILLUSTRATION

Create a hand-inked black-and-white illustration of Vaultfang, Undermarches Cannon, medium: a mottled stone-hard chitin cone that mimics a stalactite and drops once, point first, through helm and skull, in the dark fantasy world of Heraldry Skirmish, drawn the way a skilled human illustrator inked the interior art of a classic 1970s-1990s fantasy tabletop roleplaying game rulebook: dip pen, brush and India ink on paper.

FORMAT AND ISOLATION
Square 1:1 composition. Pure black ink on warm ivory-white paper, with no colour, lettering, borders, or decorative framing.
A single subject drawn whole, isolated on blank ivory-white paper: only a small patch of ground beneath it and its cast shadow, which fade out in a few sparse strokes. No background scenery.
Leave at least 8% clean white margin on every side; nothing touches or is cropped by the image edge. The silhouette must read clearly when printed about two inches wide.

COMPOSITION
Vertical view of the creature in its drop: a single stone-like cone falling point first, tiny gripping claws still splayed at its top where it let go, a few flakes of rock falling with it, its narrow shadow on a small patch of cave floor below.
Single creature, full body, three-quarter view, in a characteristic menacing pose; a small human figure for scale only if the creature is Large or Huge. Isolated on white paper with only a small patch of ground and its cast shadow; no background scenery.
Size: Medium: about human-sized.

SUBJECT DETAILS
* A single cone-shaped body mottled like stone
* Ridged chitin plates banding the cone
* Single large barbed point at the tip
* Tiny gripping claws at the top to cling to the ceiling
* No eyes, no limbs beyond the claws
Must be shown exactly:
* Looks like a stalactite at first glance

SOURCE TEXT
Depict this faithfully and invent nothing that contradicts it:
"A vaultfang clings to the cavern roof as a flawless imitation of a stalactite, a mottled cone of stone-hard chitin, and it waits. When something passes beneath, it drops, driving its single barbed point clean through helm and skull. It is a living spear that falls exactly once. Few who survive a low ceiling here ever walk easy under one again."

ARTISTIC STYLE AND ATMOSPHERE
Still stone becoming a falling spear.
Ink it like classic rulebook monster and character art: a bold, confident outer contour, clean interior lines, hatching only where form turns into shadow, and a few solid-black spot shapes for the deepest darks. Large areas stay open white paper. Simple, readable shapes over surface texture: no stippled skin, no noise, no over-rendered detail. Light falls from the upper left. Believable anatomy, armour construction, weapon grips and materials, worn and weathered. It must look drawn by hand with pen, brush and ink, not generated or rendered.

FINAL VISUAL REQUIREMENTS
Do not draw:
* Not a bat or spider.
* Do not draw legs or a face.
* No obvious monster features: at first glance it reads as stone.
Avoid photorealism, digital-render polish, hyper-detail, stippled or noisy texture, painterly shading, smooth gradients, grey wash, colour, typography, readable runes, watermarks, signatures, modern objects, cartoon or anime proportions and cropped limbs. The finished artwork should be clean, bold and readable at small size, like original ink art in a professionally printed fantasy rulebook.

STYLE REFERENCES
The attached reference images are hand-inked art by human illustrators. Match their line quality, economy of line, hatching density, amount of open white paper and finish, but do not copy their subjects or compositions.
