7.6 TREASURE TABLES - CLASSIC FANTASY PEN-AND-INK ILLUSTRATION

Create a hand-inked black-and-white illustration of Treasure in the Dying Lands is never clean: coin and relics come from hoards, vaults and the dead, in the dark fantasy world of Heraldry Skirmish, drawn the way a skilled human illustrator inked the interior art of a classic 1970s-1990s fantasy tabletop roleplaying game rulebook: dip pen, brush and India ink on paper.

FORMAT AND ISOLATION
Wide 3:2 landscape composition. Pure black ink on warm ivory-white paper, with no colour, lettering, borders, or decorative framing.
A still-life arrangement isolated on blank ivory-white paper, resting on a small surface that fades out in a few sparse strokes. No figure and no background scenery.
Leave at least 8% clean white margin on every side; nothing touches or is cropped by the image edge.

COMPOSITION
Still life on a stone slab at slight elevation: a rotted purse spilling tarnished coins beside a skeletal hand, a mud-caked crown, a cracked reliquary and a salt-crusted signet ring.
Still-life arrangement of the described objects, worn and used. Isolated on white paper with only a small patch of ground and its cast shadow; no background scenery.

SUBJECT DETAILS
* Rotted leather purse with a frayed drawstring spilling coins
* Skeletal hand with finger bones curled near the coins
* Mud-caked crown with dull gems and a bent circlet
* Cracked reliquary box with a missing hinge
* Salt-crusted signet ring on a stone
* Coins stippled to feel dull, no readable stamps
* Cobweb, grave dirt and a spilled bone button

SOURCE TEXT
Depict this faithfully and invent nothing that contradicts it:
"Treasure in the Dying Lands is never clean. Coin comes from burial hoards, failed relief columns, chapel vaults, drowned roads, damned shrines, dead merchants, broken armies, and rooms sealed by people who feared being found. A purse is often the last record of a life. A crown may have passed through dirt, blood, ash, salt water, and another thief's stomach before it reaches a living hand."

ARTISTIC STYLE AND ATMOSPHERE
Greedy and grave-soiled.
Ink it like classic rulebook monster and character art: a bold, confident outer contour, clean interior lines, hatching only where form turns into shadow, and a few solid-black spot shapes for the deepest darks. Large areas stay open white paper. Simple, readable shapes over surface texture: no stippled skin, no noise, no over-rendered detail. Light falls from the upper left. Believable anatomy, armour construction, weapon grips and materials, worn and weathered. It must look drawn by hand with pen, brush and ink, not generated or rendered.

FINAL VISUAL REQUIREMENTS
Do not draw:
* Not the Magic traditions still life or the melee weapon still life.
* No readable coin inscriptions.
* No glinting sparkle effects.
* No gore.
Avoid photorealism, digital-render polish, hyper-detail, stippled or noisy texture, painterly shading, smooth gradients, grey wash, colour, typography, readable runes, watermarks, signatures, modern objects, cartoon or anime proportions and cropped limbs. The finished artwork should be clean, bold and readable at small size, like original ink art in a professionally printed fantasy rulebook.

STYLE REFERENCES
The attached reference images are hand-inked art by human illustrators. Match their line quality, economy of line, hatching density, amount of open white paper and finish, but do not copy their subjects or compositions.
