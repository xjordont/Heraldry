7.8 MELEE WEAPONS - CLASSIC FANTASY PEN-AND-INK ILLUSTRATION

Create a gritty, old-school black-and-white ink illustration of Salvaged arms from an older wider world that reach the Dying Lands as trade goods through Saltspire and Reedmarch, in the dark fantasy world of Heraldry Skirmish, drawn the way a human illustrator inked the interior art of a late-1970s or 1980s fantasy rulebook or adventure module: brush, dip pen and India ink on paper, with the bite of a woodcut.

FORMAT AND ISOLATION
Wide 3:2 landscape composition. Pure black ink on warm ivory-white paper, with no colour, lettering, borders, or decorative framing.
A still-life arrangement isolated on blank ivory-white paper, resting on a small surface that fades out in a few sparse strokes. No figure and no background scenery.
Leave at least 8% clean white margin on every side; nothing touches or is cropped by the image edge.

COMPOSITION
Still life laid out on a sacking cloth viewed from above at an angle: a long-bladed messer, notched swordbreaker, throwing cross, bladed scourge, weighted cord and hook-and-chain, spaced evenly.
Still-life arrangement of the described objects, worn and used. Isolated on white paper with only a small patch of ground and its cast shadow; no background scenery.

SUBJECT DETAILS
* Langes messer: long single-edged blade with a nagel-style hilt
* Swordbreaker with a notched back and a heavy guard
* Throwing cross: four-armed bladed star-cross
* Bladed scourge: multiple lashes ending in small blades
* Weighted cord with a heavy ball at one end, coiled
* Hook-and-chain: a long chain ending in a barbed hook
* Burlap sack texture, a trader's tag as a blank rectangle, rope bindings
Must be shown exactly:
* Only the six named weapons

SOURCE TEXT
Depict this faithfully and invent nothing that contradicts it:
"Many of the arms below carry names from an older, wider world than the Bastions now remember: the long-bladed messer, the notched swordbreaker, the throwing cross, the bladed scourge, the weighted cord, and the hook-and-chain. They reach the Dying Lands as salvage, tribute, and trade goods, carried up the southern routes through Saltspire and bartered onward through Reedmarch. How a blade is named changes nothing about how it cuts. Each is listed here under the name the traders used."

ARTISTIC STYLE AND ATMOSPHERE
Matter-of-fact salvage with dangerous elegance.
Ink it like gritty old-school rulebook monster and character art: a bold, rough brush contour of uneven weight, heavy solid-black shadow shapes, and scratchy hatching or dry-brush drag where form turns from light into shadow. High contrast, with few in-between textures; small human imperfections such as broken lines and dry-brush streaks are welcome. It reads as a strong silhouette first and detail second: no stippled skin, no uniform micro-texture, no over-rendered surfaces. Light falls from the upper left. Believable anatomy, armour construction, weapon grips and materials, worn, scarred and weathered. It must look drawn by hand with brush, pen and ink, not generated or rendered.

FINAL VISUAL REQUIREMENTS
Do not draw:
* Not the treasure still life.
* No weapons beyond the six named, no swords of legend.
* No readable maker's marks.
* No gore or blood.
Avoid photorealism, digital-render polish, clean vector-like line, stippled or noisy texture, painterly shading, smooth gradients, grey wash or grey ink tones, airbrush, colour, typography, readable runes, watermarks, signatures, modern objects, cartoon or anime proportions and cropped limbs. The finished artwork should be gritty, bold and readable at small size and print crisply in pure black ink, like original ink art in an old-school fantasy rulebook.

STYLE REFERENCES
The attached reference images are gritty, old-school ink art by human illustrators. Match their bold brush contours, heavy solid blacks, scratchy hatching, dry-brush texture, contrast and finish, but do not copy their subjects or compositions, and draw in pure black ink only.
