THE MIRE - CLASSIC FANTASY PEN-AND-INK ILLUSTRATION

Create a hand-inked black-and-white illustration of the Mire, the southeastern swamps where rivers give out, where bogs swallowed roads and the dead drift below the walkways, in the dark fantasy world of Heraldry Skirmish, drawn the way a skilled human illustrator inked the interior art of a classic 1970s-1990s fantasy tabletop roleplaying game rulebook: dip pen, brush and India ink on paper.

FORMAT AND WHITE VIGNETTE
Wide 3:2 landscape composition. Pure black ink on warm ivory-white paper, with no colour, lettering, borders, or decorative framing.
The illustration must have a natural, irregular, hand-drawn white vignette around all four edges. The artwork gradually dissolves into blank ivory-white paper through increasingly sparse pen strokes, broken outlines and diminishing hatching.
Leave approximately 5-8% clean white margin around the perimeter, with an additional organic transition zone. Avoid digital blur, artificial feathering, or hard rectangular cropping. Keep crisp line and strong contrast at the focal point.

COMPOSITION
Water-level view along a plank walkway through a drowned village; mangroves grow through the roofs of stilt-houses, pale shapes drift beneath the surface.

SUBJECT DETAILS
* Leaning stilt-houses over dark water
* Mangrove roots bursting through a roof
* Plank walkway with missing boards
* Water that looks shallow but opens in a darker circle
* Pale drifting shapes beneath the surface (faces, hands) in fine lines
* Reeds, floating weed, hanging moss, a heron-like bird
* A lantern on a pole, unlit

HUMAN ACTIVITY
* A lone toll-ferryman poles a flat boat in the far distance, tiny

SOURCE TEXT
Depict this faithfully and invent nothing that contradicts it:
"Where the southern rivers gave out before the sea, the swamps inherited the rest, spreading across the world's south-eastern lowlands. Bogs swallowed roads. Mangroves grew through village roofs. Stilt-houses lean over water that looks shallow until it opens. Reedmarch survives by toll, ferry, leechcraft, and denial. The hag-courts hold the deep fen, and they keep older bargains than any Bastion will admit to honouring. Everyone there knows The Mire is rising. No one profits from saying it aloud. The dead drift below the walkways. Sometimes they drift back."

ARTISTIC STYLE AND ATMOSPHERE
Damp, patient and quietly rising.
Draw it with confident hand-inked line: clean outlines of varied weight made with dip pen and brush, carrying the slight natural irregularity of a human hand. Build the picture from clearly drawn shapes, not surface texture. Use hatching and crosshatching selectively, only to model form and shadow, and keep generous areas of open white paper; let distance thin to simple, lighter outline. Put detail where the eye should land and suggest the rest with a few telling strokes. No stipple fields, no noise-like texture, no tonal haze, no over-rendered surfaces. Accurate perspective, believable construction and coherent geography; the world shows withered beauty: once-grand things rusted, cracked, overgrown or hollowed by age. It must look drawn by hand, not generated or rendered.

FINAL VISUAL REQUIREMENTS
Do not draw:
* Not Reedmarch itself (no barges, council hall or toll bridges) and not Vossegar's cathedral.
* No horror close-ups: dead shapes stay ambiguous and faint.
* No lettering.
* Not the Blighted Heartwood or Titan fen scenes.
Avoid photorealism, digital-render polish, hyper-detail, uniform texture everywhere, painterly shading, smooth gradients, grey wash, colour, typography, readable runes, watermarks, signatures, modern objects, artificial symmetry and competing focal points. The finished artwork should read cleanly and print crisply, with an organically fading white vignette, like original ink art in a professionally printed fantasy rulebook.

STYLE REFERENCES
The attached reference images are hand-inked art by human illustrators. Match their line quality, economy of line, hatching density, amount of open white paper and finish, but do not copy their subjects or compositions.
